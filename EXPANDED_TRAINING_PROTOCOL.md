# Can more steep-terrain training examples reduce large misses?

This follow-up was designed after the original steep-error pilot failed on a small development subset. It compares the released point model with the same estimator trained on additional steep river sites. The 22 published held-out results apply to the frozen released model. The expanded fit reuses five formerly held-out historical events as training data.

Keep the same features, residual target, boosting configuration, and survey masks. The expanded fit uses the original four training events plus all five episodes in the separately defined earlier-archives cohort. Fit one model with the usual observation weights and one that gives each training event equal total weight. At riverine points with at least 35 ft of local raster relief, consider replacing the released prediction or averaging it equally with the expanded fit. All other predictions stay as released. Survey budgets remain identical.

Select at most one replacement on the held-out West Virginia 2016, Montana 2022, and Helene 2024 replays. These floods were scored previously for the original estimator only. Select by pooled absolute reduction in the rate of errors over five feet. Require at least 1 percentage point reduction, at most 2% higher mean absolute error, and at most 3 percentage points more large misses in any one validation flood. Freeze the selected estimator and mixture weight before opening the reserved flood outcomes.

The reserved field measurements must come from a named USGS report on a different event from any of the existing training, development, or formal benchmark panels. Attach separately documented data provenance with original field IDs and coordinates. Use the same 16 masks. Show the nearest distance to past study sites, record spatial overlap, and report paired intervals accounting for repeat masks and within-event blocks. The adoption gate requires at least two qualifying reserved events and 50 steep site examples, at least 2 percentage points fewer large errors pooled, at most 2% increased mean absolute error, no more than 3 percentage points worsening in any event, and a strictly positive lower 95% paired bound for the reduction. Report failures even if the candidate looks promising. The same-event anchor readings are available during prediction, and this study concerns post-flood survey replay.
