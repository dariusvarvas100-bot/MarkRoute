# Extra-measurement experiment

This retrospective extension asks which extra measurements improve the released MarkRoute estimates at other points. It uses the frozen gradient-boosted estimator and its original training data. The five previous point-prediction test floods and eight remote floods remain evaluation cohorts for this distinct task. Flood outcomes and coverage in the earlier point-prediction task were already known.

For each flood and mask, split within each environment. Select initial survey anchors with the released 15% rule. Divide remaining points at random into candidate and scoring pools. Environments with fewer than four remaining points do not enter this experiment. Give every policy the same starting observations, locations, terrain and named waterbody tags. Freeze the whole extra batch before revealing any new elevations. The primary budget is one measurement per eligible environment. A secondary budget adds 10% of the environment's total sites, rounded with a floor of one. Score the same untouched sites after each choice. Use 12 masks (seeds 7162–7173) per event.

The research policy greedily reduces integrated conditional variance on the scoring locations. Its smooth Matérn covariance uses either geographic distance, or geographic and terrain distance plus a same-waterbody covariance component. Freeze the variant from Florence and Michael. Compare against random, geographic farthest-first, shared terrain-kernel facility coverage, greatest estimator disagreement with bounded regression, and no additional measurement. Select the strongest same-budget control by development MAE separately at each budget. The geography-only variance policy remains a reported ablation. The covariance is a design heuristic for the nonlinear estimator.

The primary metric is pooled absolute error in feet. Report flood-level and coastal/riverine performance, errors over 5 ft, and paired percentile intervals with floods and 10 km blocks resampled. A headline claim about better site selection requires the primary policy to reduce MAE at least 10% against the frozen development control, with a strictly positive paired 95% interval and positive gain in at least 9 of 13 floods. The secondary budget is descriptive. Scoring labels are inaccessible to selectors, and only selected candidate elevations enter prediction. The replays do not model walkability, available staff, safety, travel time or survey cost.

References. [Krause, Singh and Guestrin, spatial observation design](https://jmlr.org/papers/v9/krause08a.html) · [USGS field manual on high-water marks](https://pubs.usgs.gov/tm/03/a24/tm3a24.pdf)
