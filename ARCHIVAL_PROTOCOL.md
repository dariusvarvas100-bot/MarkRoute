# Earlier inland floods and geographic overlap

This extension uses the same frozen four-event boosted estimator and the same development-selected terrain-aware kriging comparison as the earlier point-prediction studies. It also compares bounded terrain regression. The aim is to test archival river floods, with an explicit separate check at sites with steep nearby terrain.

Screen the USGS Short-Term Network catalog snapshot for events of type 1 or 2 that started from 2000 through 2013 and ended by the end of 2013. Exclude names containing test, training, shakedown, bridge, gages, study or exercise. Exclude all events already used in the three published point-prediction panels. In a separate cohort, screen every prior remote candidate excluded on geography alone. There is no new distance requirement in either cohort. Record the distance to the closest previous study site and exclude exact repeat site IDs. This makes the extension event-held-out. Sites can still be near previous sites.

Require published USGS field measurements in feet relative to NAVD88, an excellent/good/fair mark rating, Riverine environment, finite coordinates and an elevation. Keep one best-quality mark per distinct source site and per 1/600-degree grid cell. Require 30 usable sites per event after deduplication. Iowa catalog records of the 2002 June flood and 2008 May–July flood are grouped within each year as one event episode before applying the site threshold. Reuse the published terrain features and require complete raster support for each included event. Use event metadata, data quality, location, and terrain for eligibility. Record exclusions before fitting or scoring.

The primary scoring subset is all withheld riverine observations whose surrounding raster relief is at least 35 feet, using the existing 17×17 pixel 90th-minus-10th percentile statistic. Points outside the subset can still provide initial surveys. Score the full cohort as a secondary result and provide each flood's MAE and share of errors over five feet. Reuse the same 16 masks as the existing benchmark and provide paired intervals that resample floods and 10 km blocks. The high-relief outcome supports a positive result only if at least two events and 30 unique scored sites enter that subset, reported pooled MAE is at least 5% lower than both baselines, and paired intervals have positive lower bounds against both. Report all results. The external-event extension does not model field costs, access, or predict water levels before a flood.
