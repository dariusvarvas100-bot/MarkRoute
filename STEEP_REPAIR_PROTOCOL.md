# Reducing large misses on steep river terrain

The published estimator minimizes average absolute error. This study asks whether a prediction targeted at reducing errors beyond five feet improves that outcome. Measurements, terrain, the initial survey budget, and holdout masks stay fixed. Candidate changes apply only to riverine points whose local raster relief is at least 35 feet.

Train MarkRoute and the candidate estimators on the same four previous floods and 18 masks. The first candidate family estimates seven conditional quantiles of residual elevation from the same features. Sort crossed quantiles, interpolate the resulting approximate distribution, and center the 10 ft window capturing the most estimated probability. Consider applying 25%, 50%, or 100% of that estimate's difference from released MarkRoute. The second candidate family triples the training weight of steep river sites and learns the residual with either absolute or squared loss. Consider 50% and 100% blends with the released estimate. All settings in this family are fixed before fitting.

Primary outcome is the share of steep river estimates that miss by more than 5 ft. Select one candidate on the two development floods, using the lowest large-miss rate among candidates with at least 1.5 percentage points pooled improvement, pooled mean absolute error within 2% of the release, and no development flood more than 2 percentage points worse. Report all development candidates. If none passes the guardrails, report the outcome and keep the release. Freeze the qualifying candidate by script and input hashes, then evaluate the existing 22-event archive with the original 16 masks and only then any separate reserved field panel. Do not use known archive results to select between candidates. For adoption, require at least 1 percentage point pooled improvement and positive 95% lower interval for the difference on the 22-flood archive, mean absolute error within 2%, no event with at least 30 steep sites more than 3 percentage points worse, and improvement with no meaningful rise in mean absolute error on the independent reserved cohort. Report counts and paired event/spatial-block bootstrap intervals. A finite held-out sample cannot establish that future large misses are impossible.

The quantiles are used as a decision aid for this point prediction. This study does not claim calibrated prediction intervals. [Modal interval forecasting, Brehmer and Gneiting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.05709)
